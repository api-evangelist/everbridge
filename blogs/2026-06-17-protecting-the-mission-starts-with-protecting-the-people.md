Every faith-based organization is guided by a greater purpose than its buildings, programs, or operations. The article argues that faith leaders should prioritize protecting their communities through crisis communication and business continuity planning, positioning preparedness as stewardship rather than fear-based planning and the ability to reach staff, volunteers, and congregants during disruptions.
